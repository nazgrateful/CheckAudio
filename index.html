          Energy by frequency band

          Splits the audio into layers you can listen to on their own, and shows the frequencies each one covers. It also lists any steady tones running through the whole file. Those are usually things added to the recording, such as hum, whine, test tones or watermarks.

          Layer spectra

          Hover for values

          Each line is one layer's frequency content: low pitches on the left, high on the right, and higher lines mean more energy. Where a line rises above the others, that layer dominates those pitches. For example, percussive peaks in the lows are drums, and a tonal line rising in the mids is melody or voice.

          Layers

          These layers are separated with signal processing, not AI. Tonal, percussive and noise always add back up to the original, but the split is a rough one. For true vocal, drum and bass stems you need an AI separator such as Demucs.
